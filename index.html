How it works

A good video holds a lot of knowledge, but search engines read text. A blog article built from the video lets people find your knowledge through search, read it quickly, and then watch the video for more. Writing that article by hand from a transcript takes hours, and a raw transcript published as is reads badly.

Video to Article turns a video into a proper article. You paste a link or upload a file. The service transcribes the video, finds the structure of what you said and writes an article in your voice: an introduction, sections with headings, short paragraphs, a list of key points and a conclusion.

The article is not a word-for-word transcript. Repetition and filler are removed, examples are kept, and the order follows the logic of the topic. You can choose the length — a short summary post or a longer guide — and the tone, from conversational to formal.

Screenshots from the video are picked for the sections they illustrate and placed in the article, each with alternative text. The service suggests a title, a meta description and a main phrase that matches the topic, so the article is ready for search engines without extra work.

You review the article in an editor, change anything you want and export it — as formatted text to paste into any blog, or as a file for your blogging platform. The video can be embedded at the top so readers can switch to watching.

Each plan includes a number of videos per month and a maximum video length. Articles stay in your library, so you can come back and update them later.

Creators with a back catalog can process older videos first: popular videos without articles are often the quickest way to bring more search visitors to a blog.

A typical week looks like this. You publish a new video, paste its link, and within minutes the article draft is ready. You read it, adjust a paragraph or two, check the title and the meta description, and publish it on your blog with the video embedded at the top. Readers who find the article through search can read the key points and watch the video for the details.

Over months, a blog built this way becomes a second entry point to your channel or business: each article brings readers from search who might never have found the video directly.
